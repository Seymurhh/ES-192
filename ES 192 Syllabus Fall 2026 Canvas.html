Course Description

How does an engineer select a material for a particular component or application? This course provides a systematic, rational methodology for answering that question, with a specific focus on material selection in mechanical design.

There are now thousands of materials to choose from, new materials are constantly being discovered, the properties of existing materials are being improved, ever more sophisticated engineering systems are being devised, and new manufacturing processes—including additive manufacturing—are being developed. At the same time, constraints such as element scarcity, environmental impact, and sustainability requirements increasingly influence the designer's choices.

This course describes how each of these developments influences the choices facing a mechanical designer today. The pedagogical approach combines:

  • Lectures — theory, methodology, and case studies
  • Materials selection software — materials database exploration, Ashby chart generation, and computer-aided selection
  • COMSOL Multiphysics simulations — structural and thermal analysis to complement analytical methods
  • Hands-on laboratory work — laser cutting, 3D printing, and silicone mold casting in the Active Learning Laboratories
  • A structured mini-project (Weeks 1–5) followed by an open-ended team design project (Weeks 7–13)

Course Objectives

After completing the course, students should be able to:

  1. Translate design requirements for a mechanical component into materials selection criteria in terms of function, constraints, objectives, and free variables.
  2. Derive and apply materials indices from the mechanics of a component's function, and use them with Ashby property charts and materials databases to screen and rank candidate materials.
  3. Handle multiple constraints and conflicting objectives using active constraint methods, trade-off surfaces, and penalty functions.
  4. Evaluate the role of shape through shape factors and their interaction with materials indices.
  5. Select manufacturing processes appropriate to the chosen material, geometry, and production volume, understanding how processing affects material properties.
  6. Design with hybrid materials—composites, foams, sandwiches, and lattice structures—using rules of mixing and bounds methods.
  7. Use COMSOL simulations to predict structural behavior and validate material selection decisions against experimental results.
  8. Assess environmental impact through life-cycle assessment, embodied energy analysis, and eco-selection methods.
  9. Apply hands-on manufacturing skills (laser cutting, 3D printing, silicone mold casting) to realize material and process selections in physical prototypes.
  10. Communicate materials selection decisions through clear written reports and oral presentations.

Detailed Weekly Schedule

Two lectures per week + one lab section per student. Readings refer to Ashby, 6th Edition.

Part 1: Materials and Process Selection — The Basics (Weeks 1–5)

Week 1 — Introduction to Materials and Design

Readings Chapters 1 and 2
Lecture Topics
  • Why materials selection is necessary. Thousands of materials, orders of magnitude in property ranges.
  • The design process: types of design, function-material-shape-process interaction.
  • Families of engineering materials: metals, polymers, ceramics, composites, natural materials.
  • Material properties and their units: mechanical, thermal, electrical, optical, environmental.
  • Introduction to materials databases and selection software: navigating property data, generating Ashby plots.
  • Case study: Devices to open corked bottles (Ch 1.7).
Lab ALL Orientation & Mini-Project Launch
Safety training (30 min). Equipment tour (laser cutters, 3D printers, casting station). Mini-project briefing: the Hybrid Cantilever Challenge — teams design a multi-material cantilever that maximizes load/mass. Team formation (groups of 3). Functional decomposition: beam, clamp, connectors. Complete Design Requirements Tables for each component. Generate Ashby plots (E vs. ρ), overlay material index M = E1/2/ρ, select candidate materials from available stock.
MS Concept in Lab Translation of design requirements: Function, Constraints, Objectives, Free Variables; materials indices for beams

Week 2 — Materials Property Charts and the Selection Strategy

Readings Chapters 3 and 4
Lecture Topics
  • Ashby property charts: reading log-log plots, material families as clusters, property correlations.
  • Key charts: modulus–density, strength–density, modulus–strength, fracture toughness–strength, thermal conductivity–electrical resistivity.
  • The selection strategy: translation → screening → ranking → documentation.
  • Deriving materials indices from mechanics: tie, beam, plate, shell in bending and tension.
  • Attribute limits. The structural index. Computer-aided selection.
Lab Laser Cutting: Structural Components
Laser cutter training (20 min: operation, DXF/SVG file prep, kerf compensation). Teams cut structural components (beam, flanges, clamp plates) + standard test coupons (100 × 20 mm) from 2+ sheet materials. Baseline materials testing: 3-point bend test on coupons — measure deflection under known loads, calculate flexural modulus, compare to database values. This data justifies the material choice for the structural beam.
MS Concept in Lab Property-based screening: measuring material properties and comparing to database; using data to justify beam material selection
Assignment 1 Due Getting started: materials property plots (Ashby charts), material index derivation for a simple component, short written report on a material of your choice. COMSOL orientation exercise.

Week 3 — Materials Selection Case Studies

Readings Chapter 5 (selected sections: 5.2 Oars, 5.3 Telescopes, 5.6 Flywheels, 5.7 Springs, 5.11 Pressure Vessels, 5.18 Heat Sinks)
Lecture Topics
  • Applying the full selection methodology to real components.
  • Case studies: materials for oars (stiff, light beams), telescope mirrors (low thermal distortion), flywheels (maximum energy storage), springs (maximum elastic energy), safe pressure vessels (leak-before-break), heat sinks (thermal conductivity + stiffness).
  • Lightweighting: evolution of aircraft materials (wood → aluminum → CFRP). The Boeing 787 Dreamliner.
  • Wind turbine blade material selection.
Lab 3D Printing: Connectors & Mold Patterns
FDM printing training (20 min: slicer, infill, orientation, supports). Teams design and print functional components (joints, brackets, clamp adapters) + mold pattern for casting (with draft angles, parting features). Filament selection: PLA (stiff), PETG (tough), TPU (flexible grip pads). Print orientation experiment: two identical small beams printed flat vs. on-edge — bend test both to demonstrate layer-direction anisotropy. Longer prints queued to run after session.
MS Concept in Lab Process-property relationships: how print orientation creates anisotropy; Design for AM (infill, supports, filament selection)

Week 4 — Processes and Their Effect on Properties

Readings Chapters 6 and 7
Lecture Topics
  • Classifying manufacturing processes: shaping (casting, forming, machining, molding, AM), joining, finishing.
  • How processing changes microstructure and properties: process-property trajectories.
  • Systematic process selection using matrices. Process limits: size, shape complexity, tolerance, surface finish, material compatibility.
  • Process cost modeling: material cost, tooling cost, capital, overhead, batch size effects.
  • Video: Making a soda can.
Lab Silicone Mold Casting & Assembly
Casting training (15 min: silicone mold-making from 3D-printed patterns, resin mixing, degassing, cure). Teams make silicone molds around their patterns (backup pre-made molds available). Cast components in selected resin: rigid PU (structural), flexible PU (damping/grip), epoxy (strong bond), or fiber-filled resin. While parts cure, begin assembling non-cast components — dry-fit, plan joining methods (bolts, adhesive, press-fit). Discussion: shapes enabled by casting, defects and their effects.
MS Concept in Lab Process selection for complex geometry; casting constraints and defects; resin selection as a material choice
Assignment 2 Due Materials selection for a mechanical component (aircraft wing or wind turbine blade): Design Requirements Table, derive performance index, Ashby chart screening, written analysis of material evolution. Process cost comparison problem.

Week 5 — Additive Manufacturing & Mini-Project Testing

Readings Chapter 8 (Additive Manufacturing)
Lecture Topics
  • Additive manufacturing processes: FDM, SLA, SLS, binder jetting, DED.
  • Materials for AM: polymers, metals, ceramics. Properties of AM materials vs. conventionally processed.
  • Design for additive manufacturing: topology optimization, lattice structures, part consolidation.
  • AM and the environment: material waste, energy consumption, lifecycle comparison.
  • The business case for AM: when does AM make economic sense?
Lab Load Testing & Validation
Final assembly. Weigh and measure cantilevers. Test protocol: clamp to fixture, load tip incrementally, record deflection at each step, identify F_max at 10 mm deflection, continue to failure. Calculate P = F_max / m. Post class leaderboard. Key learning moment: compare Week 1 Ashby predictions to actual results — discuss discrepancies (joint failures, defects, boundary conditions, material variability). Class-wide discussion: which design choices mattered most?
MS Concept in Lab Predicted vs. actual performance — validating Ashby analysis with experimental data; understanding why models differ from reality
Mini-Project Report Due Team report (3–5 pages): design rationale with Ashby plots, material/process justification per component, load-deflection data, predicted vs. actual comparison, failure mode analysis, lessons learned.

Projects

Mini-Project: The Hybrid Cantilever Challenge (Weeks 1–5)

Working in teams of 3, students design, fabricate, and test a multi-material cantilever beam that must support the maximum load at a fixed overhang distance while minimizing total weight. Each component uses a different manufacturing process — laser cutting for structural members, 3D printing for connectors and custom joints, and silicone mold casting for complex-geometry load-bearing components.

Every material and process choice must be justified using the Ashby methodology: Design Requirements Tables, materials indices, and property charts. Students compare their Week 1 predictions to actual test results, learning firsthand where models succeed and where real-world factors (joints, defects, boundary conditions) create discrepancies. The performance metric is P = Fmax / m (maximum load at 10 mm deflection divided by total mass).

The mini-project is intentionally structured so that the manufacturing skills, materials testing experience, selection methodology, and design-build-test workflow learned each week transfer directly to the group project.

Group Project (Weeks 7–13)

Teams of 3 select, design, build, and test a project of their own choosing that demonstrates materials selection principles in a mechanical design context. Each project must include:

  1. A clearly defined design problem with Function, Constraints, Objectives, and Free Variables
  2. A formal materials selection analysis using the Ashby methodology
  3. COMSOL simulation of at least one structural/thermal aspect
  4. Fabrication of a physical prototype using ALL facilities
  5. Quantitative testing of at least one performance metric
  6. A final presentation (12–15 min) and written report (10–15 pages)

Project proposals are due in Week 8. The instructor will meet with each team regularly for project brainstorming and guidance.
