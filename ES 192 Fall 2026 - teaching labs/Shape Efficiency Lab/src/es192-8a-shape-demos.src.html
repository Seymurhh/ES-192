Load vs deflection

Sources and models

Equations and page numbers refer to Ashby, Materials Selection in Mechanical Design, 6th edition (Elsevier, 2025).

Columns

  • Euler load Fc = n²π²EI/L² (Eq 11.14, p.389), pinned ends n = 1. Shape factor φ = 12I/A² (Eq 11.3, p.380); tube 3r/πt, solid 3/π (Table 11.3), so the load ratio is r/t.
  • Real columns start slightly bent. The demo uses a starting bow of L/1000 that grows as 1/(1 − F/Fc); first yield is where axial plus bending stress reaches σy (the Perry–Robertson idea). After first yield the column collapses.
  • Sections: EN 10219 circular hollow section 60.3 × 2.9 mm; solid bar of equal area 25.8 mm. S355: E = 210 GPa, σy = 355 MPa.

Thin walls

  • Aluminium 6061-T6, E = 69 GPa, σy = 250 MPa, area 150 mm². The wall-kink stress 0.145 E t/r is a teaching model chosen so yield and kinking meet at Ashby's limit φmax ≈ 2.3(E/σf)½ (Eq 11.15a, p.393).

Cells

  • Aligned cells ψ = ρs/ρ (Eq 11.38, p.406). Foam E = (ρ/ρs)²Es (Eq 13.18, p.470) and σpl = 0.3(ρ/ρs)3/2σs (Eq 13.20, p.471). Aligned-cell strength σ = (ρ/ρs)σs.
  • Cell-wall solid ≈ 1500 kg/m³, 35 GPa, read from the cellulose bubble on Fig 13.34. Loads are given as a fraction of the load that breaks the solid bar. Wood is about half as strong along the grain in compression as in tension, so the squeezed face fails first.

Lattices

  • Octet truss (Fig 13.21): Maxwell M ≥ 0 (Eq 13.3, p.453), E ≈ ⅓(ρ/ρs)Es (Eq 13.27, p.473), collapse σ ≈ ⅓(ρ/ρs)σs (Eq 13.28, p.474) or strut buckling 0.2(ρ/ρs)²Es (Eq 13.29), whichever is lower.
  • Open (body-centred) lattice: M < 0, E ≈ (ρ/ρs)²Es, σpl ≈ 0.3(ρ/ρs)3/2σs. Ti-6Al-4V as printed: Es = 115 GPa, σs ≈ 1000 MPa (typical laser powder-bed values).
  • Squash in the 3D view is drawn 8× larger than the elastic strain; the ratio between the two lattices is true.

Corrugation

  • φe = (2a+t)²/t² and φf = (2a+t)/t (Eqs 12.8–12.9, p.436) are ideal values for a section through the wave peaks; real gains are a little lower, and deep corrugations can also buckle locally at the crest. S280GD galvanized sheet, σy = 280 MPa.

Microscope views

  • Grain and atom views are drawings of the mechanisms, not simulations. Strain is exaggerated so it can be seen. Atom views show one slip plane side-on; the real plane sits at about 45° to the load, where the shear stress on it is largest.
  • Mechanisms: slip on {110} planes in ferrite and dislocation pinning by carbon (steel); {111} slip and Mg–Si (β″) precipitates in 6061-T6; α′ martensite, partly melted powder and pores in printed Ti-6Al-4V; cellulose chains joined by hydrogen bonds in wood cell walls; zinc-coating cracking on galvanized sheet.
